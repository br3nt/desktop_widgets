A Sonique-inspired, skinnable workspace: independent instruments that can come together as one tangible object, while keeping their own digital lives.

Docking study accepted. The current magnetic interaction and connection feedback are the approved baseline. Fine tuning of distances and easing is deferred. These notes describe that baseline; they do not change it.

01 / The instrument, not the music player

An interaction study for agentic workflows, potentially spanning Command Center, Jarvis, GitDiscuss and a prototyping tool. This is not a music player or desktop pet. The names and activity are illustrative; no services are connected.

Sonique is the reference for sculpted silhouettes and physical machinery: smoky green-grey metal, curved shoulders, inset cyan displays, lime indicators, lens-like buttons and distinctive blue/green end details. Ornament should frame useful controls, not consume their space.

The intended family has small, medium and large forms. This experiment isolates a narrower question: can two compact instruments fuse convincingly, separate cleanly and hold another widget in a working drawer? A complete size-morphing system is not yet implemented.

02 / Two layers, two kinds of behaviour

Physical housing & controls

The “chrome”: casing, bevels, buttons, knobs, rails, drawers and connectors. Something you could plausibly fabricate and hold. Motion communicates weight, alignment, resistance and attachment.

Digital display surfaces

The programmable screens inside the object: task views, conversations, navigation, readouts and on-screen controls. Their content changes without requiring the surrounding device to change shape.

Both layers are interactive. This means physical versus digital, not decoration versus functionality. A physical button can change a digital page; a workflow event might eventually expose a physical drawer.

03 / Authored connections, not universal stretching

A skin author chooses which connections exist and what they permit. Some silhouettes should remain standalone. Proximity does not make arbitrary edges compatible.

Either joined nameplate moves the assembly. Display pages and running/paused states survive joining and separation. The drawer belongs to Jarvis; its loaded widget stays with it when the capsules separate or the drawer closes.

Physical attachment is not workflow wiring. Joining housings does not share agent context, grant permissions, trigger work or establish a data connection.

Design mode reveals sockets, display boundaries and the bay. It is an authoring aid, not yet a skin editor. Multiple groups, arbitrary module counts and saved layouts remain future work.

04 / A connection should keep its promise

Capsules and drawer bays use the same boundaries. Feedback depends on current proximity, not approach history. Move away and approach again: the same feedback must return.

Distance to aligned poseLightRelease outcome
Outside 120 pxNo proximity glow. Leaving the boundary eases the light out.No connection.
120 px → above 40 pxA dim glow eases on, then grows toward medium brightness with closeness. Moving away dims it.Nearby, but not ready to connect.
40 px or lessEases to steady full brightness, independent of distance inside this boundary.Ready: releasing completes the connection.

These are stage-space distances between the pointer-driven widget pose and its aligned docking pose, before magnetic attraction offsets the visible widget—not the apparent gap between curved edges.

Transitions are quick but perceptible in both directions. Readiness updates immediately; easing affects only the light. Status text distinguishes nearby from ready while the glow catches up. Attraction, guidance and latch resistance remain separate from visual feedback.

Release is intentional

Nameplates move the joined group. Pull the small vertical grip right to separate capsules. Checks can be deliberately pulled out or ejected. Breakaway gestures have their own thresholds; these are not different docking radii.

Escape cancels dragging. Join, Separate, Insert and Eject provide keyboard-operable alternatives. Reduced-motion mode removes timed movement and glow interpolation. Perceived force is simulated through movement; there is no physical haptic feedback.

Implementation values — recorded, not new tuning targets
  • Shared proximity/capture radii: 120 / 40 px.
  • Approach glow: 0.18 + 0.44 × ((120 − distance) / 80)^0.8. Capture target: 1.
  • Glow uses a 40 ms exponential time constant, not a 40 ms total transition. It settles over several time constants.
  • Spring stiffness 400, damping 40; captured pull 0.88. Attraction and glow are independent.
  • Capsule 390 × 130 px; joined offset 316 px; gap between useful displays 22 px.
  • Visible release bar 6 × 24 px, transparent pointer target 22 × 36 px.
  • Deliberate release thresholds: shell 72 px outward; cartridge 46 px movement.

05 / What the experiments taught us

  • Preserve useful centres; relocate ornament. Fusion is not two complete cases stuck together. Inner end details retract into shared casing.
  • Do not turn the release control into another empty module. The bulky separator became a narrow, centred vertical grip with a larger invisible grab area.
  • Glow is a promise, not just atmosphere. Dim-to-medium means proximity; full means release will capture. Both connection types follow the same rule.
  • No hidden penalty for changing direction. Post-escape suppression was removed so a second approach behaves like the first.
  • Preserve the tactile baseline when tuning visuals. Changing an indicator should not silently change magnetic behaviour.
  • Draw each detached object as one coherent stack. Shell, caps and display must occlude together. Painting all shells before all caps caused overlap artefacts.
  • Joining must not steal adjacent controls. Transparent module bounds must not block neighbouring displays or buttons.

06 / Boundaries & next steps

Working now

Capsule fusion and separation, group movement, magnetic guidance, shared proximity feedback, independent display pages, simulated run/pause controls, a sliding drawer, cartridge insertion/ejection and socket visibility in design mode.

Still a browser study

Counts, tasks and logs are simulated; no external actions occur. Layout does not persist across reloads. Small labels and compact targets need an accessibility pass before production. The desktop-oriented stage scrolls horizontally on narrow screens.

Native macOS is a separate proof

Borderless floating windows, desktop click-through, focus, Spaces, multiple monitors and Dock-aware placement are not implemented. Transparency in this page does not demonstrate native click-through behaviour.

The intended permission strategy is progressive enhancement: useful manual anchors without extra permissions, with optional enhanced Dock awareness for people who enable the required access. The native mechanism and permissions still need validation. Dynamic positions beside the real Mac Dock remain an intended feature.

Proposed next: make the displays earn their space

Keep the accepted housing interaction stable and prototype one simulated workflow: review a proposed change → agent needs a decision → respond in the drawer → inspect the result → return to a compact view. Use it to decide when a digital page change is enough and when the housing should expose more space. This is a recommendation, not an implemented or approved workflow specification.

Procedural composition can wait

Paul Merrell’s graph-grammar work is a possible future direction for assembling authored pieces. This prototype uses fixed rules, not wave-function collapse or a graph-grammar engine. Any future system also needs constraints for readable content, accessible controls and understandable navigation—not only compatible geometry.
